# Editorial Tone Guide for Tour Narration

## Purpose

This guide defines how tour narration should sound for the main customer segments of the product. It is intended for prompt design, content review, voice direction, and future quality evaluation.

## Core Principle

The narration must feel useful while walking, emotionally engaging without being theatrical by default, and credible enough for culturally curious travelers.

The platform should support more than one tone family. Not every tour should sound mysterious, and not every tour should sound academic. The best experience is controlled tonal flexibility within a consistent quality bar.

## Tone Pillars

### 1. Clear

- Easy to follow while moving through a city.
- Short enough to process on foot.
- Avoid overloaded sentences and excessive detail stacking.

### 2. Human

- Sounds like a thoughtful guide, not a textbook or an encyclopedia.
- Speaks directly to the listener when appropriate.
- Uses natural transitions between stops.

### 3. Credible

- Avoids making uncertain claims sound definitive.
- Prefers grounded historical context over invented drama.
- Treats serious sites with respect.

### 4. Atmospheric

- Helps the listener notice place, mood, texture, and context.
- Uses sensory detail selectively.
- Creates immersion without slowing comprehension.

## Primary Tone Modes

### Mode A: Curious and vivid

Best for:

- mystery tours
- hidden history
- legends and anecdotes
- food and street culture

Characteristics:

- energetic but not cartoonish
- inviting and memorable
- slightly playful when appropriate
- focused on hooks, contrast, and surprise

Good example:

"At first glance this corner looks quiet, but for centuries it carried the noise of trade, rumor, and political ambition."

Avoid:

- exaggerated suspense in every paragraph
- clickbait phrasing
- slang that makes the tour feel disposable

### Mode B: Serious and cultivated

Best for:

- history tours
- architecture tours
- museums and heritage districts
- religious, civic, or memorial sites

Characteristics:

- calm, elegant, and precise
- rich in context without becoming dense
- respectful of place and historical weight
- intellectually satisfying without sounding academic for its own sake

Good example:

"This facade is important not because it is decorative, but because it captures the civic ambitions of the city at a decisive moment in its history."

Avoid:

- stiff lecture voice
- dry date recitation
- fake solemnity

### Mode C: Warm and practical

Best for:

- general city tours
- first-time visitors
- broad mixed-interest routes

Characteristics:

- approachable and helpful
- balanced between context and orientation
- easy for a wide audience to enjoy

## Tone Selection by Tour Type

| Tour Type | Default Tone | Secondary Note |
|-----------|--------------|----------------|
| History | Serious and cultivated | Add vivid details to avoid dryness |
| Architecture | Serious and cultivated | Highlight form, material, and urban meaning |
| Food and culture | Curious and vivid | Keep practical and sensory |
| Mystery or legends | Curious and vivid | Stay believable |
| Intro city walk | Warm and practical | Blend with city-specific flavor |

## Buyer Persona Alignment

### Mateo, the Explorer

- Wants both intrigue and rigor.
- Enjoys historical gossip, but also serious tours.
- Best served by a balance of Mode A and Mode B depending on route theme.

### Sofia, the Efficient Planner

- Wants clarity, structure, and cultural value.
- Prefers Warm and practical with moments of Serious and cultivated.

### Lucas and Ana, the Slow Weekend Couple

- Wants atmosphere, romance, and memorable detail.
- Prefers Warm and practical with touches of Curious and vivid.

### Elena, the Cultural Purist

- Wants depth, respect, and intellectual trust.
- Prefers Serious and cultivated.

## Writing Rules

- Lead with what matters at the stop.
- Avoid opening every stop with the same framing pattern.
- Mention dates only when they help interpretation.
- Prefer one strong insight over three weak facts.
- Keep transitions natural and directional.
- Do not overexplain obvious visuals.
- Do not use filler excitement.

## Audio Rules

- Write for the ear, not the page.
- Avoid long subordinate clauses.
- Keep names and concepts pronounceable in sequence.
- Leave enough rhythm for walking and orientation.
- End segments with a sense of closure or forward movement.

## Quality Check Questions

- Would this sound good through headphones while walking?
- Is the tone appropriate for the site and theme?
- Does it feel informed without sounding academic or robotic?
- Would the listener remember at least one idea from this stop?
- Is the voice respectful where it needs to be?

## Bottom Line

The editorial goal is not to force one universal style. It is to deliver the right level of energy, seriousness, and clarity for each route while preserving a consistent sense of quality.
