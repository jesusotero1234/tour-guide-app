# Pricing Strategy

## Purpose

This document captures the current pricing model for the Tour Guide product and explains why each offer shape fits the target customer segments.

## Pricing Principles

- Keep entry pricing low enough for impulse purchase.
- Reduce trust friction before asking for a larger payment.
- Increase average order value with multi-route bundles.
- Match the payment model to real tourist behavior on the street.

## 1. Standard Price Per City Route

### Recommended range

- `$4.99 USD` to `$9.99 USD`
- In Europe: `EUR4.99` to `EUR9.99`

### Offer definition

The user buys a single route in a specific city, such as a historical walk, architecture route, or themed tour.

### Why it works

- It behaves like an impulse purchase.
- The price is low enough to feel casual, especially for travelers already spending on food, transport, and attractions.
- If a tourist sees a QR code near a monument and the route costs around `$5`, the barrier to purchase is very low.
- Fast browser access plus Apple Pay or Google Pay makes the purchase feel almost instant.

### Best fit

- Mateo, the Explorer
- tourists already near the attraction
- users discovering the product through QR codes, posters, hotels, or Airbnb placements

### Positioning line

"Cheaper than a coffee and dessert, and far more memorable."

## 2. Pay-What-You-Want Unlock

### Base model

Give away the first `20%` of the route, usually the first `2 stops`.

At the third stop, lock the rest and ask for a voluntary payment starting at:

- `$2.99 USD`

### Why it works

- It removes distrust before payment.
- The user can verify that the website loads fast, the interface feels comfortable, the narration is strong, and the voice is pleasant.
- Once the value is proven, the user is much more likely to pay.
- It acts as a product demo without requiring a separate trial flow.

### Strategic value

- lowers first-purchase resistance
- helps unknown brands earn trust quickly
- creates a natural conversion point once curiosity is already activated

### Best fit

- skeptical first-time users
- QR traffic with low prior brand awareness
- users comparing your experience against free generic content online

### Positioning line

"Listen first. Unlock the rest only if it feels worth it."

## 3. City Pass Strategy

### Recommended range

- `$14.99 USD` to `$19.99 USD`

### Offer definition

Instead of selling one route, sell a bundle such as:

- `Complete Historical Madrid Pass`
- includes `3 to 4 routes`
- example bundle:
  - Madrid de los Austrias
  - Barrio de las Letras
  - Ghost Tour at Night
  - Optional food or architecture route

### Why it works

- It increases average order value.
- It feels like savings compared with buying each route separately.
- It fits travelers staying in the city for more than one day.
- It reframes the product from a one-off purchase into a weekend cultural companion.

### Best fit

- repeat listeners during the same trip
- weekend travelers
- couples and city-break visitors
- users who want flexibility across multiple moods or time slots

### Positioning line

"For the price of one trendy drink, get a full weekend of cultural exploration."

## Recommended Offer Architecture

### Entry layer

- single route at `$4.99` to `$9.99`

### Trust-building layer

- first `2 stops` free
- unlock remainder from `$2.99`

### Value-maximizing layer

- city pass at `$14.99` to `$19.99`

This structure gives the product three monetization paths:

- immediate impulse conversion
- low-friction sample-to-paid conversion
- higher-ticket bundle conversion

## Segment Mapping

| Segment | Best Offer | Why |
|---------|------------|-----|
| Mateo, the Explorer | Single route or pay-what-you-want unlock | Likes spontaneity and low friction |
| Sofia, the Efficient Planner | Single route or city pass | Values clarity and bundled convenience |
| Lucas and Ana, the Slow Weekend Couple | City pass | Good for multi-day discovery and shared use |
| Elena, the Cultural Purist | Premium single route or curated pass | Pays for quality and depth if trust is high |

## Product Implications

- The first two stops must be strong enough to sell the rest.
- Checkout must be extremely fast on mobile.
- The paywall moment should feel natural, not aggressive.
- The city pass should be explained in terms of value, flexibility, and savings.
- The interface should clearly show how many routes or stops are included.

## Go-To-Market Implications

- QR placements are especially important for the single-route and freemium model.
- Hotels, Airbnb hosts, and local partners are strong channels for city passes.
- Messaging should adapt to context:
  - near a monument: fast access and impulse value
  - online ads: free sample plus cultural quality
  - partner channels: bundle value and city coverage

## Open Questions to Validate Later

- Whether `$4.99` or `$5.99` is the best default entry point.
- Whether the free portion should always be `2 stops` or vary by route length.
- Whether the unlock should be fixed-price or truly user-selected above `$2.99`.
- Whether some premium routes deserve pricing above the standard range.
- Whether the city pass should be limited by time, by route count, or by city.

## Bottom Line

The strongest pricing system is not one single price. It is a three-layer offer stack:

- low-friction single route
- trust-building freemium unlock
- higher-value city pass

Together, these cover impulse buyers, cautious first-time users, and higher-intent travelers without forcing one rigid payment path on everyone.
